RESTAURANT FOOD SAFETY GUIDELINES – COUNTY PUBLIC HEALTH DEPARTMENT
Edition: 2026 Compliance Handbook
Applicable To: All food service establishments including restaurants, cafes, bakeries, food trucks, school cafeterias, and temporary food vendors.

INTRODUCTION
Food safety is a central pillar of public health. These guidelines outline the standards that must be followed by all commercial food establishments to prevent contamination, maintain hygiene, and ensure the well-being of customers. All restaurant managers and food handlers must familiarize themselves with these regulations and ensure compliance at all times.

SECTION 1 — PERSONAL HYGIENE REQUIREMENTS
1.1 Handwashing Protocol  
- Hands must be washed:
  * Before beginning food preparation  
  * After handling raw meat, poultry, seafood, or eggs  
  * After using the restroom  
  * After touching the face, hair, phone, or personal items  
  * After handling trash, cleaning supplies, or dirty dishes  
- Handwashing must be performed using warm water and soap for at least 20 seconds.  
- Hand sanitizer is not a substitute for handwashing. It may be used only after handwashing.

1.2 Employee Clothing and Protective Gear  
- All employees must wear clean clothing and aprons.  
- Hair restraints are required at all times.  
- Jewelry on hands and wrists should be minimized to avoid contamination.  
- Gloves must be worn when handling ready-to-eat foods.

1.3 Illness and Work Restrictions  
- Employees exhibiting symptoms of vomiting, diarrhea, fever, or contagious illnesses must be excluded from food handling tasks.  
- The Person-In-Charge (PIC) must maintain a written log of illness exclusions.

SECTION 2 — FOOD STORAGE REQUIREMENTS
2.1 Temperature Control  
- Cold foods must be held at 41°F or below.  
- Hot foods must be held at 135°F or above.  
- Frozen foods must remain frozen until ready for thawing.  
- Temperature logs must be maintained and verified twice per shift.

2.2 Cross-Contamination Prevention  
- Raw foods must be stored below ready-to-eat foods.  
- Cutting boards must be color-coded or labeled for specific uses.  
- Containers must be sealed and clearly labeled.  
- Chemicals must never be stored above or near food items.

2.3 Dry Storage  
- All ingredients must be stored at least 6 inches above the floor.  
- Shelves should be cleaned monthly or more frequently as needed.  
- Food items must be stored in pest-resistant containers.

SECTION 3 — THAWING AND COOLING PROCEDURES
3.1 Approved Methods for Thawing  
- Under refrigeration at 41°F or below  
- Under cold running water not exceeding 70°F  
- In microwave followed by immediate cooking  
- As part of the cooking process

3.2 Cooling Requirements  
Foods must be cooled from:
- 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours  
- 70°F to 41°F within 4 additional hours  
Total cooling time may not exceed 6 hours.

Shallow pans, ice baths, and blast chillers should be used to accelerate safe cooling.

SECTION 4 — SANITATION AND CLEANING STANDARDS
4.1 Cleaning Schedule  
Restaurants must maintain a written cleaning schedule. The following items require daily cleaning:
- Prep surfaces  
- Cutting boards  
- Sinks  
- Grill surfaces  
- Microwave interiors  
- Floor drains  
- Food-contact utensils

Weekly cleaning tasks include:
- Exhaust hoods  
- Refrigeration gaskets  
- Storage shelves  
- Beverage dispenser drip trays  
- Trash room and bin surfaces

Monthly cleaning tasks include:
- Ceiling vents  
- Behind large appliances (grill, fryers, ovens)  
- Drain traps  
- Ice machine interior surfaces

4.2 Approved Sanitizers  
- Quaternary ammonia (Quat) at 200–400 ppm  
- Chlorine bleach at 50–100 ppm  
- Hot water sanitization at 171°F or higher

Test strips must be used to verify sanitizer strength.

SECTION 5 — EQUIPMENT MAINTENANCE
5.1 Food Thermometers  
- Must be calibrated weekly  
- Must be cleaned and sanitized before and after each use  
- Logs must document calibration

5.2 Refrigeration Units  
- Temperature monitoring twice per shift  
- Gaskets inspected bi-weekly  
- Units must be free from excessive frost  

5.3 Deep Fryers  
- Oil must be replaced based on clarity and particulate levels  
- Fryers must be cleaned according to manufacturer guidelines

SECTION 6 — WASTE AND GARBAGE CONTROL
6.1 Indoor Waste  
- Lids must remain closed at all times  
- Trash should be removed every 4 hours or as needed  
- Waste bins must be cleaned daily

6.2 Outdoor Waste  
- Dumpsters must remain sealed  
- Grease containers must be inspected daily for leaks  
- Waste areas must be free of debris to prevent pest attraction

6.3 Grease Trap Maintenance  
- Traps should be emptied based on usage volume  
- Overflow can attract pests and violate sanitation laws

SECTION 7 — PEST CONTROL STANDARDS
7.1 Preventive Measures  
- All openings such as doors and windows must have tight seals  
- Cracks in walls or floors must be repaired promptly  
- Food must never be stored directly on the floor

7.2 Signs of Pest Activity  
- Droppings  
- Gnaw marks  
- Live insects  
- Grease trails  
- Damaged packaging  
- Nesting materials  

7.3 Contractor Requirements  
- Pest control services must occur at least monthly  
- Reports must be retained for a minimum of 12 months  
- Facilities must document internal monitoring findings

SECTION 8 — FACILITY MANAGEMENT RESPONSIBILITIES
8.1 Person-In-Charge (PIC) Duties  
- Ensure staff compliance  
- Maintain logs and documentation  
- Verify corrective actions  
- Oversee food safety training  

8.2 Staff Training  
Employees must be trained in:
- Personal hygiene  
- Safe food temperatures  
- Approved thawing methods  
- Cross-contamination prevention  
- Proper equipment sanitation  
- Chemical safety

SECTION 9 — EMERGENCY PROCEDURES
9.1 Power Outage  
- Discard foods held above 41°F for more than 2 hours  
- Document product losses  
- Notify health department if closures are required

9.2 Sewage Backup  
- Immediate closure required  
- Professional cleaning must occur  
- Inspector must verify corrections before reopening

9.3 Water Interruption  
- Facility must close until potable water is restored  
- Bottled water may be used for limited operations only with prior approval

CONCLUSION
These guidelines establish the minimum hygiene and food safety standards required in all food service establishments. Consistent adherence to these rules is necessary for preventing foodborne illness and ensuring public health.
