COUNTY RESTAURANT CLEANING AND SANITATION STANDARDS  
2026 Regulatory Compliance Handbook  
Issued by: County Environmental Health & Sanitation Division  

INTRODUCTION  
Restaurants, food vendors, and commercial kitchens must adhere to strict cleaning and sanitation standards to prevent the spread of foodborne illness. This document forms the official county guidelines for cleaning frequency, chemical usage, equipment maintenance, water safety, waste disposal, and emergency response. Failure to follow these standards may result in citations, fines, or temporary closure of the establishment.

This handbook must be reviewed quarterly by the Person-In-Charge (PIC), and all employees must receive training on relevant sections.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1 — DAILY CLEANING REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 Prep Areas  
All preparation surfaces must be cleaned and sanitized after each task and at least every four hours during continuous use. Cutting boards must be washed using hot, soapy water followed by sanitizing using an approved solution. Wet boards must be air-dried before reuse.

1.2 Cooking Equipment  
Cooking equipment—including grills, fryers, flat-tops, ovens, sandwich presses, and steamers—must be wiped at the end of each shift. Grease buildup must be completely removed from grill edges, fryer walls, and underneath stovetops. Grease accumulation is a major contributor to pest attraction and fire hazards.

1.3 Refrigerators and Freezers  
Spills or leaks must be cleaned immediately. Shelves must be wiped daily using food-safe detergent. Refrigerator door gaskets must be inspected for mold, tears, and residue accumulation. Freezer condensation must be monitored to prevent frost buildup impacting door closure.

1.4 Sinks  
Handwashing sinks must remain unobstructed. Prep sinks must be cleaned after each use. Three-compartment dishwashing sinks must be washed and sanitized at least once daily, including faucet handles.

1.5 Floors  
Kitchen floors must be swept and mopped daily. Mop water must be changed as soon as visibly dirty or after cleaning waste areas. Mop sink drainage must be free of standing water.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2 — WEEKLY CLEANING REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Exhaust Hood and Filters  
Kitchen exhaust hoods accumulate grease from steam and smoke. Filters must be removed weekly and washed. Persistent residue requires degreaser and hot water soak.

2.2 Storage Shelves  
Dry storage shelves must be sanitized weekly. Unlabeled items must be discarded. Shelves must be organized to prevent food from being stacked in ways that restrict airflow.

2.3 Drains  
All floor drains must be scrubbed with brushes weekly. Drain covers must be removed to clean internal components.

2.4 Trash Room  
Indoor trash storage areas must be deep cleaned weekly. Floors must be sanitized and residues from food scraps removed.

2.5 Beverage Machines  
Coffee brewers, soda machines, and water dispensers must be flushed weekly. Drip trays must be cleaned and sanitized.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — MONTHLY CLEANING REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Behind Large Appliances  
Areas behind ovens, fryers, grills, and refrigerators must be cleaned monthly. Dust and grease buildup attracts pests and impedes airflow.

3.2 Ceiling Vents and Panels  
Ceiling vents must be vacuumed monthly. If grease buildup is visible, vents must be removed and washed thoroughly.

3.3 Storage Reassessment  
All dry goods must be rotated monthly. Expired herbs, spices, and packaged foods must be discarded.

3.4 Ice Machine  
Ice tray compartments must be deep-cleaned monthly. Mold residue is a common issue in neglected machines.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4 — FOOD TEMPERATURE AND HOLDING REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Cold Holding  
All refrigerated foods must be kept at 41°F or below. Cut leafy greens, tomatoes, melons, and dairy-based sauces are considered highly perishable and must remain below this threshold. Cold holding temperatures must be verified twice per shift.

4.2 Hot Holding  
Foods kept hot must remain at 135°F or above. Steam tables must be monitored frequently. Warmers must not be overloaded to ensure even heat distribution.

4.3 Cooling Requirements  
The following cooling timeline is mandatory:
- 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours  
- 70°F to 41°F within 4 hours  
Food exceeding these thresholds must be discarded. Cooling logs must be maintained.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5 — CHEMICAL SAFETY AND STORAGE
------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Approved Chemicals  
Approved sanitizers include quaternary ammonium compounds, chlorine-based cleaners, and iodine solutions. Detergents must be food-safe for kitchen equipment. Bleach must be diluted appropriately.

5.2 Chemical Placement  
Chemicals must never be stored above food items or equipment. Spray bottles must have clearly marked labels. Unlabeled chemicals must be discarded immediately. Chemical shelving must be cleaned weekly.

5.3 Sanitizer Strength Testing  
Sanitizer buckets must be tested at least twice daily using appropriate test strips. Strength requirements:
- Quat: 200–400 ppm  
- Chlorine: 50–100 ppm  

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6 — WATER SAFETY AND PLUMBING
------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Potable Water Requirements  
Restaurants must have access to potable water at all times. If water service is interrupted, restaurant operations must cease until restored.

6.2 Hot Water Availability  
Hot water temperature for dishwashing must reach at least 120°F. If temperatures cannot be reached, dishwashing sinks cannot be used until water is restored.

6.3 Drainage  
Drain blockages must be resolved immediately. Standing water in floor drains is considered a critical violation due to contamination risk.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7 — WASTE DISPOSAL REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Indoor Waste  
Waste bins must:
- Remain covered  
- Be emptied frequently  
- Be cleaned and sanitized daily  

Food scraps must never accumulate around bin edges. Wet waste bags must be double‑bagged.

7.2 Outdoor Waste  
All dumpsters must remain closed. Grease trap containers must be inspected daily for overflow. Waste areas must remain free of debris to prevent pest attraction.

7.3 Hazardous Waste  
Oil and grease disposal must follow county environmental guidelines. Grease containers must be placed on stable surfaces away from foot traffic.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8 — PEST CONTROL REGULATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Contractor Requirements  
Restaurants must use licensed pest control contractors. Visits must occur monthly or quarterly depending on facility size. Reports must be retained for one year.

8.2 Internal Monitoring  
Employees must document:
- Live insect sightings  
- Droppings  
- Gnaw marks  
- Grease trails  
- Damaged packaging  
- Nesting materials  

8.3 Mitigation  
If pest activity is observed, immediate actions include:
- Deep cleaning affected areas  
- Removing contaminated food  
- Repairing wall or floor cracks  
- Scheduling contractor visit within 48 hours  

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9 — EMERGENCY CONDITIONS REQUIRING TEMPORARY CLOSURE
------------------------------------------------------------

The following situations require **immediate closure** of a restaurant:

9.1 Sewage Backup  
Any sewage overflow or backup inside the facility requires immediate closure until full sanitization and reinspection.

9.2 Power Outage  
If food temperatures cannot be maintained due to power loss, closure is required. Foods that exceed safe temperature limits must be discarded.

9.3 Water Service Interruption  
If potable water is unavailable, the facility must close. Bottled water may only be used for limited functions with prior health department approval.

9.4 Rodent Infestation  
Presence of live rodents constitutes a critical violation. Signs of rodents include droppings, nesting material, grease marks, and gnawing. Closure remains until pest control clears the facility.

9.5 Structural Hazard  
Ceiling collapse risk, unstable shelving, or unsafe flooring conditions require immediate closure until repairs are verified by inspectors.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 10 — INSPECTOR CHECKLIST FOR ROUTINE VISITS
------------------------------------------------------------

Inspectors must review:
- Temperature logs  
- Sanitizer strength measurements  
- Equipment cleanliness  
- Pest control logs  
- Employee hygiene practices  
- Waste disposal condition  
- Drainage systems  
- Compliance with cooling procedures  
- Chemical storage
- Date labels and FIFO rotation  
- Gasket condition  
- Ice machine cleanliness  
- Vent condition  

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 11 — MANAGEMENT RESPONSIBILITIES
------------------------------------------------------------

The Person-In-Charge (PIC) must:
- Maintain all logs  
- Conduct employee training  
- Document violations and corrective actions  
- Ensure pest control scheduling  
- Oversee cleaning tasks  
- Respond to inspector warnings promptly  
- Report structural issues immediately  
- Verify throat clearance of grease traps  
- Check handwashing supply stock  

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 12 — STAFF TRAINING REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

Training must address:
- Hygiene practices  
- Cleaning schedules  
- Cross-contamination risks  
- Food temperature rules  
- Chemical handling  
- Emergency response  
- Pest control basics  

Training must be documented with employee signatures and dates.

------------------------------------------------------------
CONCLUSION  
These cleaning and sanitation standards form the backbone of safe restaurant operations. All staff must follow these guidelines daily, weekly, and monthly. Failure to comply may result in citations, fines, or temporary facility closure.
