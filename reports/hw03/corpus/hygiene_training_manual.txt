HYGIENE TRAINING MANUAL FOR FOOD SERVICE EMPLOYEES  
County Department of Environmental Health  
2026 Standardized Employee Training Module  

OVERVIEW  
This hygiene manual provides detailed guidelines for all employees who handle food in commercial establishments. These guidelines are intended to reduce contamination risks, promote safe food handling, and ensure consistent compliance with county food safety laws. All employees must complete this training before performing any food preparation tasks.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1 — INTRODUCTION TO FOOD SAFETY
------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 What is Food Safety?  
Food safety refers to the handling, preparation, and storage practices that prevent foodborne illnesses. Safe food handling protects customers, the restaurant, and the community.

1.2 Employee Responsibilities  
Employees must:
- Follow personal hygiene requirements  
- Maintain clean workstations  
- Report contamination events  
- Follow glove and handwashing rules  
- Prevent cross-contamination  

1.3 Person-In-Charge (PIC) Overview  
The Person-In-Charge is responsible for ensuring training, verifying compliance, documenting corrective actions, and responding to inspector feedback.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2 — HANDWASHING AND GLOVE REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Handwashing Basics  
Proper handwashing is a critical component of hygiene:
- Use warm water and soap  
- Scrub hands for at least 20 seconds  
- Rinse thoroughly  
- Dry with paper towels  
Handwashing must occur before beginning food prep and after contamination.

2.2 When Handwashing is Required  
Employees must wash hands:
- After using the restroom  
- After touching hair, face, clothing, phones  
- After handling raw meat, poultry, or eggs  
- After touching trash or cleaning supplies  
- Before switching between tasks  
- Before applying gloves  

2.3 Glove Rules  
Gloves are required when handling ready-to-eat foods.  
Gloves must be changed:
- After touching raw foods  
- After touching non-food items  
- When torn or damaged  
- At least every 4 hours during continuous use  

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — PERSONAL CLEANLINESS
------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Clothing  
Employees must wear clean uniforms or aprons. Clothing that becomes soiled must be replaced immediately.

3.2 Hair Restraints  
Hair restraints must be worn at all times. Hair nets, hats, or tied-back coverings are acceptable. Loose hair poses a contamination risk.

3.3 Jewelry  
Jewelry can harbor bacteria:
- No rings except plain bands  
- No bracelets  
- No dangling items that may contact food  

3.4 Illness Policies  
Employees must report symptoms of diarrhea, vomiting, fever, or jaundice. The PIC must exclude ill employees from food handling duties.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4 — CLEAN WORK ENVIRONMENT
------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Maintaining Prep Areas  
Prep tables must remain clean and sanitized. Wiping cloths must be stored in sanitizer buckets with appropriate concentration levels. All utensils must be sanitized after each use.

4.2 Cutting Boards and Equipment  
Cutting boards must be sanitized after each use. Deep grooves in boards require replacement. Equipment such as mixers, blenders, slicers, and processors must be cleaned thoroughly after each task.

4.3 Storage Practices  
Food must be stored at least 6 inches above the floor to prevent contamination. Containers must be clearly labeled and sealed securely. Ready-to-eat foods must always be kept above raw ingredients.

4.4 Temperature Awareness  
Employees must understand safe temperature ranges:
- Cold foods: 41°F or below  
- Hot foods: 135°F or above  
- Cooling from 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F within 4 hours  

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5 — PREVENTING CROSS-CONTAMINATION
------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Raw vs Cooked Foods  
Raw chicken, beef, seafood, and eggs must be handled separately from ready-to-eat foods. Separate utensils, cutting boards, and containers must be used.

5.2 Handling Utensils  
Utensils must be washed, rinsed, and sanitized at regular intervals. Knives must not be placed in sinks or on cutting boards after use without washing.

5.3 Chemical Storage  
Chemicals must never be stored near food. Cleaning sprays and sanitizers must be labeled and stored away from prep areas.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6 — FOOD PROTECTION PRACTICES
------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Protecting Ready-to-Eat Foods  
Ready-to-eat foods must remain covered when not actively prepared. Sneeze guards must be used for buffets. Employees must avoid bare-hand contact.

6.2 Cooling and Reheating  
Cooling must follow county standards. Food reheated for hot-holding must reach at least 165°F. Leftovers must be cooled quickly using shallow pans or ice baths.

6.3 Preventing Spoilage  
Signs of spoilage include:
- Off-odors  
- Discoloration  
- Mold  
- Dryness or cracking in sweets  
Employees must report food spoilage immediately.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7 — HYGIENE DURING BUSY PERIODS
------------------------------------------------------------

Busy periods increase the risk of contamination. Employees must:
- Switch gloves frequently  
- Follow time-based cleaning schedules  
- Avoid rushing food prep  
- Keep spills cleaned immediately  

Employees must never skip handwashing during busy hours.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8 — WASTE AND DISPOSAL PRACTICES
------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Indoor Waste  
Waste bins must remain closed at all times. Food scraps must never accumulate around bin edges. Bins must be emptied frequently.

8.2 Outdoor Waste  
Outdoor waste bins and dumpsters must be sealed. Grease traps must be checked for overflow and cleaned regularly.

8.3 Mop Sink and Drain Areas  
Drain areas must remain free of standing water. Mop sinks must be cleaned daily.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9 — TRAINING VERIFICATION AND DOCUMENTATION
------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 Training Records  
Training logs must include employee signatures, dates, and completed modules. Managers must maintain training records for at least 12 months.

9.2 Corrective Actions  
Managers must document corrective actions when employees fail to follow hygiene rules. Employees must re-attend training sessions if repeated violations occur.

9.3 Ongoing Education  
Employees must receive refresher training annually. Updates to food safety laws must be communicated promptly.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 10 — SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------

Employee hygiene is essential to safe food operations. Restaurants must ensure that staff maintain proper handwashing, glove usage, attire, work area cleanliness, temperature awareness, and waste management.

Consistent hygiene protects customers and supports compliance with county regulations.
